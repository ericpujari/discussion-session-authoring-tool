# Preparing for the Worst (June 23+25) 🟢

## Title

- Preparing for the Worst

## Parent blurb

This week’s session focuses on two important life skills: **preparation** and **resilience.** Students will explore real-world scenarios (and one imaginative one) where things go wrong — from financial emergencies to reputational risks. For each situation, they’ll discuss whether to prepare "a little" or "a lot," and defend their choices. The goal? Help students think critically about risk, decision-making, and how to bounce back when plans don’t go perfectly.

**ALEX**

In Synthesis, and in life, things can always go wrong. But we can lower that impact with two main approaches: preparation, and resilience. Preparation lets you strengthen yourself “before” trouble hits. And resilience? It lets you bounce back “after” trouble hits. So, after each scenario, we’ll give you some tips for preparation and resilience. Which ones are the most helpful? That’s up to you. 

**FLORENCE:**

To practice this concept, you’ll see six ways things can go wrong. Really wrong. For each one, you’ll have two options: one where you prepare **a little** and one where you prepare **a lot**.

After you pick, give your reasons, and then listen to the ideas of your teammates. Not everyone will agree, and that’s okay. How much to prepare, and what to prepare for? That’s an individual decision. But either way, the goal is the same — to be ready for whatever life throws at you. In a moment, you’ll meet your team.

### Scenario 1: Preparing for the Financial Worst 2-6

Sometimes unexpected expenses happen — like needing to replace a broken phone. If you didn’t have any savings, you might be out of luck. How much should you prepare for money troubles, and why?

Prepare a little: Spend your money however you like while you’re young.

![noun-spend-8158908.webp](Preparing%20for%20the%20Worst%20(June%2023+25)%20%F0%9F%9F%A2/noun-spend-8158908.webp)

Prepare a lot: Save half of everything you get just in case of emergency.

![noun-save-money-8301323.webp](Preparing%20for%20the%20Worst%20(June%2023+25)%20%F0%9F%9F%A2/noun-save-money-8301323.webp)

`Challenge Questions:`

- Could saving too much stop you from having fun now?
- What’s harder: saving a little or a lot? Why?
- Is there a smart way to split saving and spending?

`Reflection`

Preparation Tip #1: Set small short-term goals instead of huge long-term goals.

### Scenario 2: Preparing for the Physical Worst 6-10

Accidents happen during sports, biking, or even just playing outside. If you aren’t careful, and even if you are, you could still get really hurt. How much should you prepare for physical injury, and why?

Prepare a little: Don’t choose super-dangerous activities, but otherwise join right in.

![noun-list-2197241.webp](Preparing%20for%20the%20Worst%20(June%2023+25)%20%F0%9F%9F%A2/noun-list-2197241.webp)

Prepare a lot: Wear a helmet and pads, and stretch before playing hard.

![noun-list-8341031.webp](Preparing%20for%20the%20Worst%20(June%2023+25)%20%F0%9F%9F%A2/noun-list-8341031.webp)

`Challenge Questions:`

- How much safety gear is too much?
- Do big injuries only happen in high-impact activities?
- Is it possible to be too careful when playing?
- What’s the worst thing that could happen if you didn’t prepare at all?

`Reflection`

Preparation Tip #2: Think through what might go wrong, and have a backup plan ready.

### Scenario 3: Preparing for the Social Worst 10-14

Making new friends or speaking up in a group can be scary. If you don’t prepare, you might freeze up, or miss chances to meet great people. How much should you prepare for social situations, and why?

Prepare a little: Be yourself and just say whatever comes to your mind.

![noun-conversation-1883006.webp](Preparing%20for%20the%20Worst%20(June%2023+25)%20%F0%9F%9F%A2/noun-conversation-1883006.webp)

Prepare a lot: Practice conversation starters and think of questions to ask people.

![noun-practice-7433828.webp](Preparing%20for%20the%20Worst%20(June%2023+25)%20%F0%9F%9F%A2/noun-practice-7433828.webp)

`Challenge Questions:`

- What’s the biggest risk of not preparing socially?
- Can you practice too much and sound unnatural?
- Which takes more courage: preparing or just going for it?
- Would you help a friend prepare for a big conversation?

`Reflection`

Preparation Tip #3: If you don't understand something, ask early so you don't get stuck later.

### Scenario 4: Preparing for Monsters 15-19

Imagine your town is warned about possible attacks from giant monsters like Godzilla or a sudden zombie invasion. If you aren’t ready, you might get trapped without a plan. How much should you prepare for monsters, and why?  ;) 

Prepare a little: Hope it never happens, keep living normally, and figure it out if monsters show up.

![noun-fence-8327912.webp](Preparing%20for%20the%20Worst%20(June%2023+25)%20%F0%9F%9F%A2/noun-fence-8327912.webp)

Prepare a lot: Build a safe shelter, gather food, create monster defense plans, and run practice escape drills.

![noun-fence-8222952.webp](Preparing%20for%20the%20Worst%20(June%2023+25)%20%F0%9F%9F%A2/noun-fence-8222952.webp)

`Challenge Questions:`

- What’s one monster escape plan you would definitely have?
- Would preparing for monsters also help with other emergencies?
- Where’s the safest place to go from all monsters?
- Could preparing too much take away from your normal life?

`REFLECTION`

Resilience Tip #1: Focus on small wins when things go badly.

### Scenario 5: Preparing for the Reputational Worst 19-23

Posting something online or saying something without thinking can damage the way people see you. You could upset people, lose friends, or have something you regret stay online forever. How much should you prepare for your own reputation, and why?

Prepare a little: Do what you want and don’t care about what other people think.

![noun-emotional-intelligence-7723728.webp](Preparing%20for%20the%20Worst%20(June%2023+25)%20%F0%9F%9F%A2/noun-emotional-intelligence-7723728.webp)

Prepare a lot: Always consider the consequences of your words and actions.

![noun-thoughtful-7723734 (1).webp](Preparing%20for%20the%20Worst%20(June%2023+25)%20%F0%9F%9F%A2/noun-thoughtful-7723734_(1).webp)

`Challenge Questions:`

- How long should people think before they post on social media
- Can overthinking your reputation make you too cautious?
- Have you ever known someone who posted something they regretted?
- Does being careful mean you’re not being yourself?

`Reflection`

Resiliency Tip #2: You can’t control everything, but you CAN control your mindset, your effort, and your choices.

### Scenario 6: Preparing at Synthesis 23-27

Not having good teamwork and communication skills can make it hard to get into good programs, get scholarships, or have choices about the jobs you want. You might have fewer options and have to work harder to catch up. How much should you prepare for this at Synthesis, and why?

Prepare a little: Have fun, play games, and maybe you’ll learn something as you go.

![Proxima Prize.webp](Preparing%20for%20the%20Worst%20(June%2023+25)%20%F0%9F%9F%A2/Proxima_Prize.webp)

Prepare a lot: Set weekly goals for yourself and reflect on what worked and what didn’t.

![portfolio.webp](Preparing%20for%20the%20Worst%20(June%2023+25)%20%F0%9F%9F%A2/portfolio.webp)

`Challenge Questions:`

- How should Synthesis students balance fun with future goals?
- What’s one small habit you can work on that would make a big difference?
- Can you EVER really prepare for Synthesis?

`Reflection`

Resiliency Tip #3: When you feel stuck or upset, a quick walk or break can reset your brain.

### Reflection 27-28

Resilience lets you bounce back AFTER trouble hits.
Which of the three resilience tips do YOU think is most important for the future? Why?

Think about what went right, not just what went wrong.

Focus on what you can control; don’t dwell on what you can’t.

Take a short break and a deep breath, then reset and try again.
