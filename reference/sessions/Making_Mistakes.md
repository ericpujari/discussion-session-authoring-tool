# Making Mistakes (May 19+21) 🟢

## Title

- Making Mistakes

## Parent blurb

Everyone makes mistakes — but not everyone agrees on what to *do* about them. Should we avoid them at all costs? Learn from them? Admit them? Or hide them? In this session, students will analyze six different “true/false” statements about mistakes. But rather than give them hypothetical examples, we’ll ask them to come up with their own. In this session, it’s not enough to have an idea — students will also need support for them.

Having ideas is easy. But explaining ideas? That’s the hard part. And that’s what today is about. This week’s scenarios, from supercollaborator Rama, will be different than usual. That’s because  we won’t give you “any examples”. Instead, you’ll have to come up with your own. Because to have an idea means supporting that idea, and if you can’t come up with any examples, your idea may not be what you think it is. 

---

 Even though we can all agree that we “make” mistakes, we may not all agree on how, or why, or what to do afterward. And even though this concept is complex, each scenario will force you into only two choices: true, or false. Why? Because everyone will come up with “some” examples. But which examples will be the strongest? That’s what we’ll find out. Now then, it’s time to meet your team. 

### Scenario 1: Admitting Mistakes 2-6

True or false: it is better to always admit your mistakes than to never admit them.

True

![True.webp](Making%20Mistakes%20(May%2019+21)%20%F0%9F%9F%A2/True.webp)

False

![False.webp](Making%20Mistakes%20(May%2019+21)%20%F0%9F%9F%A2/False.webp)

`challenge questions`

What is an example of when it’s BAD to admit a mistake?

What is an example of when it’s GOOD to admit a mistake?

On your team, which examples are making the most sense?

“Always” and “never” are extreme options. Do any examples prove these wrong?

`reflection`

Your turn: do you tend to admit your mistakes? Or hide them?

### Scenario 2: Consequences for Mistakes 6-10

True or false: if people make accidental mistakes that affect others, they should receive less consequences than people who do things on purpose.

`challenge questions`

How can you prove something was accidental or on purpose?

If someone got hurt, does it really matter how?

What if a person makes a LOT of “accidental” mistakes?

Is carelessness a mistake? Or a choice?

`reflection question`

Your turn: have you ever been affected by someone else’s mistake? What happened?

### Scenario 3: Reflecting on Mistakes 10-14

True or false: we should always reflect back on all our mistakes.

`challenge questions`

What’s one example that makes this false?

What’s one example that makes this true?

Are there more examples for TRUE or FALSE?

`reflection question`

Your turn: how often do you reflect back on your mistakes? A lot? Or a little?

### Halftime 14-15

Halftime. Your goal today is to use examples to support your ideas. How’s it going so far?

Everyone is using good examples.

Sometimes, we’re doing this. Other times, not.

It’s hard to think of examples each time.

`reflection`

`IF EVERYONE` Great! Having solid examples means you have solid ideas.

`IF SOMETIMES` Keep working! Ideas can just float away. Examples tie them down.

`IF DIFFICULT` It IS hard, for sure. It’s also important. Think about it: if you can’t come up with an example, do you really even have an idea?

### Scenario 4: Solutions to Mistakes 15-19

True or false: mistakes have solutions.

`challenge`

Is this true of ALL mistakes?

How do you define “solution” here?

Is there an example of a mistake with no solution?

Is there an example of a solution that could resolve all mistakes?

`reflection question`

Your turn: name one mistake you were able to find a solution to.

### Scenario 5: Mistakes and Age 19-23

True or false: the older people get, the less mistakes they make.

`challenge questions`

Can you name an older person who still makes a LOT of mistakes?

Does everyone agree on what a “mistake” is? Or is that an opinion?

Are a few BIG mistakes different than a lot of SMALL mistakes? 

Do kids and adults make different mistakes? Like what? 

`reflection question`

Your turn: do you make MORE mistakes than you did when you were younger? Or LESS?

### Scenario 6: Synthesis Supercollaborators 23-27

True or false: supercollaborators don't make as many mistakes as other Synthesis students do.

`challenge questions`

What mistakes have you seen supercollaborators make?

What mistakes do supercollaborators tend NOT to make?

In general, why do you think people make MORE or LESS mistakes?

`reflection question`

Your turn: are you more aware of your mistakes OUTSIDE of Synthesis or INSIDE of Synthesis?
