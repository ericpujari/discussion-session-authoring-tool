# Hidden Costs (Feb 10+12) ✅

## Title

Hidden Costs

## Parent blurb

Every decision carries a hidden risk. Do you confront or stay silent? Lead or listen? Innovate or play it safe? The right choice isn’t always obvious — but the wrong one has consequences. In life, leadership, and learning, trade-offs shape everything. So it isn’t just what you choose; it’s also what you sacrifice. Join us this week for an interesting conversation with other bright kids from around the world.

Florence

All choices involve consequences. And while our brains can imagine most of the consequences, there are ones we can’t see as clearly. These are called “hidden costs.” Today, you’ll see six scenarios, each with three options. But here’s the thing. Each option has a hidden cost. But we won’t tell you what it is. The best teams? Will spend time trying to figure it out. In a moment, you’ll meet your team. 

### Scenario 1: Fairness vs. Flexibility 2-6

Your younger sibling wants to borrow your favorite jacket. You normally don’t trust them with your things, but they promise to be careful. What do you do?  What are the hidden costs of your decision?

Say no – It’s your jacket and your decision. 

![S1 Say No.webp](Hidden%20Costs%20(Feb%2010+12)%20%E2%9C%85/S1_Say_No.webp)

Set conditions – Let them borrow it, but make strict rules.

![S1 Set Conditions.webp](Hidden%20Costs%20(Feb%2010+12)%20%E2%9C%85/S1_Set_Conditions.webp)

Give in – Let them borrow it without making a big deal.

![S1 Give in.webp](Hidden%20Costs%20(Feb%2010+12)%20%E2%9C%85/S1_Give_in.webp)

`challenge questions`

Could refusing to share hurt your relationship in the long run?

Would them breaking your rules create even more conflict?

If you let them borrow it, do you think they’ll appreciate it or take it for granted?

`reflection question`

Self-check: did you contribute an idea in that scenario?

### Scenario 2: Strategy vs. Sportsmanship 6-10

You're playing a friendly game of chess at school. Your opponent makes a mistake, but they don’t notice. What do you do? What are the hidden costs here?

Stay silent – Take advantage of their mistake and win.

![S2 Stay Silent.webp](Hidden%20Costs%20(Feb%2010+12)%20%E2%9C%85/S2_Stay_Silent.webp)

Tell them – it’s a friendly game; let them fix their mistake.

![S2 Tell them.webp](Hidden%20Costs%20(Feb%2010+12)%20%E2%9C%85/S2_Tell_them.webp)

Ask an adult – let them decide what’s right.

![S2 Ask Referee.webp](Hidden%20Costs%20(Feb%2010+12)%20%E2%9C%85/S2_Ask_Referee.webp)

`challenge questions`

If you win by staying silent, will it feel like a fair victory?

Would you still correct their mistake if it was a tournament?

Does asking an adult mean you don’t trust your own thinking?

`reflection question`

Self-check: did you react to the ideas of your teammates in that scenario?

### Scenario 3 Inclusion vs. Loyalty 10-14

You spend a lot of time with a close friend, but a new student seems lonely. You consider inviting them to hang out. What are the hidden costs? What do you do? 

Stick with your friend – Keep your usual plans and don’t invite them.

![S3 Stick with.webp](Hidden%20Costs%20(Feb%2010+12)%20%E2%9C%85/S3_Stick_with.webp)

Include them – Invite the new student and hope it goes well.

![S3 Include them.webp](Hidden%20Costs%20(Feb%2010+12)%20%E2%9C%85/S3_Include_them.webp)

Split time – Hang out with the new student separately.

![S3 Split Time.webp](Hidden%20Costs%20(Feb%2010+12)%20%E2%9C%85/S3_Split_Time.webp)

`challenge questions`

Could sticking with your friend make the new student feel unwelcome?

What if including the new student causes tension with your close friend?

Is it possible to balance two friendships, or will one get less attention?

`reflection question`

Self-check: have you asked a question in the first half?

### Scenario 4: Confidence vs. Self-Doubt 15-19

You studied for a big math test but still feel nervous. Right before the test, you hear classmates say it's really hard. What goes through your head? What consequences are hiding there?

Trust yourself – Remind yourself you studied and stay confident.

![S4 Trust.webp](Hidden%20Costs%20(Feb%2010+12)%20%E2%9C%85/S4_Trust.webp)

Double-check everything – Skim everything and change thinking as needed.

![S4 Double Check.webp](Hidden%20Costs%20(Feb%2010+12)%20%E2%9C%85/S4_Double_Check.webp)

Ask for reassurance – Talk to a friend before the test to ease your nerves.

![S4 Reassurance.webp](Hidden%20Costs%20(Feb%2010+12)%20%E2%9C%85/S4_Reassurance.webp)

`challenge questions`

Can overconfidence lead you to miss important details on the test?

Would double-checking make you second-guess yourself?

Would asking for reassurance from others make you more or less nervous?

`reflection question`

Team check: did each person get a chance to contribute?

### Scenario 5: Independence vs. Guidance 19-23

Your parent wants to help with your complicated final science project, but you want to do it yourself. What are the hidden costs? What should you do? 

Accept help – Let them assist with the project.

![S5 Accept help.webp](Hidden%20Costs%20(Feb%2010+12)%20%E2%9C%85/S5_Accept_help.webp)

Do it alone – Politely refuse and handle everything yourself.

![S5 Do it alone.webp](Hidden%20Costs%20(Feb%2010+12)%20%E2%9C%85/S5_Do_it_alone.webp)

Set boundaries – Let them help, but only in specific ways.

![S5 Set boundaries.webp](Hidden%20Costs%20(Feb%2010+12)%20%E2%9C%85/S5_Set_boundaries.webp)

`challenge questions`

If you accept help, will the project still feel like your work?

If you do it alone, what are the consequences?

Would setting boundaries leads to tension with your parent?

`reflection question`

Team check: did everyone stay on topic for that scenario?

### Scenario 6: Game Blamer  23-27

One of your teammates is getting upset and blaming others for making mistakes. You want to deal with the situation. What do you do? What are the hidden consequences?

Call for a coach - explain the situation and ask for help working through it. 

![S6 Call Coach.webp](Hidden%20Costs%20(Feb%2010+12)%20%E2%9C%85/S6_Call_Coach.webp)

Tell them to stop - be direct and clear, but still calm. 

![S6 Tell them to stop.webp](Hidden%20Costs%20(Feb%2010+12)%20%E2%9C%85/S6_Tell_them_to_stop.webp)

Lead by example - be positive to your team in the face of negativity.

![S6 Lead by example.webp](Hidden%20Costs%20(Feb%2010+12)%20%E2%9C%85/S6_Lead_by_example.webp)

`challenge questions`

If a person is upset, what’s the best way to approach them?

Does encouragement overcome blame? 

When do you (and don’t you) call for a coach? 

What would you do at Synthesis?

`Reflection question`

Team check: was this a “successful” conversation? Why or why not?
