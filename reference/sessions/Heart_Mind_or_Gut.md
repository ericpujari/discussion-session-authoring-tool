# Heart, Mind, or Gut?

## Title

- Heart, Mind, or Gut?

## Parent blurb

When making a big decision, where do you turn? Your gut, which reads a situation before you can explain why? Your heart, which knows what you love? Or your mind, which weighs the facts? Every day — with friends, at school, on teams — we lean on all three voices, sometimes without even realizing it. This week, students explore six scenarios that put gut, heart, and mind in competition with each other. No right or wrong answers here — just interesting conversations with kids from around the world.

**Alex**

In discussions, it's tempting to just announce which voice you'd trust — gut, heart, or mind — and move on to the next person. But the real value is in the *why*. This week, push yourself to fully explain your reasoning, even for your gut calls. "I just felt it" is a start, not a finish. At the end of the session, we'll ask how it went.

**Florence**

We all have three voices whispering to us when we make a choice. Your gut, which picks up on things before you've fully worked them out. Your heart, which knows what you love. And your mind, which works through the facts. All three are useful. All three can also be wrong. So which one deserves your trust, and when? Today, you'll see six scenarios that put gut, heart, and mind in competition with each other. There's no universal winner here — just three ways of knowing, and six chances to think about when each one serves you best. In a moment, you'll meet your team.

### Scenario 1: New Kid at Lunch 2-6

A new student sits alone at lunch every day, headphones on. Your table is usually full, but there's room for one more. What do you do, and why?

1. Follow your gut: Smile and say hi as you pass — their posture reads as "not today," so you hold off on inviting them.

[icon: a glancing eye]

2. Follow your heart: Invite them to sit with you now — they seem lonely, and welcoming them would make you happy.

[icon: a figure with open arms]

3. Follow your mind: Plan it out first — how to make room and introduce them smoothly — then invite them once you're ready.

[icon: a checklist]

`challenge questions`

What's the risk of misreading someone's body language?

Does waiting for "the right plan" risk losing the moment?

Would your answer change if you were the new student?

Which voice usually wins for you in social situations?

`reflection question`

Self-check: did you explain WHY you chose gut, heart, or mind — not just which one?

### Scenario 2: Weekend Plans 6-10

It's your turn to pick your family's weekend plan. Nobody's said it, but you sense everyone's stressed and cooped up. A hike could help everyone unplug. Baking is what you truly love. A museum trip fits the family's schedule and budget best. What do you choose, and why?

1. Follow your gut: Choose the hike — you sense everyone's stressed and cooped up, even though no one's said so.

[icon: a winding path]

2. Follow your heart: Choose baking — it's what you love, and it always makes you happy.

[icon: a mixing bowl]

3. Follow your mind: Choose the museum trip — it fits your family's time and budget best.

[icon: a balanced scale]

`challenge questions`

How do you know if your read on your family's mood is accurate?

Does "practical" always mean "best" for a family?

What's the risk of always picking what makes YOU happy?

If your whole family disagreed with your pick, would you change your mind?

`reflection question`

Self-check: did you listen closely when a teammate chose differently than you?

### Scenario 3: Science Fair Topic 10-14

You're picking a science fair topic. One idea reminds you of a project that almost worked last year — you sense a twist on it could go further. Another is something you're genuinely passionate about, even if it's more work. A third is the topic most likely to win, based on past years. What do you choose, and why?

1. Follow your gut: Pick the idea that reminds you of something that almost worked before — you sense this twist could go further.

[icon: a lightbulb with a spark]

2. Follow your heart: Pick the topic you're passionate about — you'll care about it, even through the extra work.

[icon: a heart with a spark]

3. Follow your mind: Pick the topic most likely to win, based on what's worked before.

[icon: a trophy]

`challenge questions`

How do you tell the difference between a good hunch and wishful thinking?

Is passion or strategy a bigger predictor of success?

What would you do if your heart and your gut disagreed?

Which voice would your teacher probably recommend?

`reflection question`

Your turn: which voice do you usually listen to first in real life?

### Scenario 4: Team Presentation 15-19

Your group must decide how to present to the class. You remember how much energy a live demo got last term, and sense this topic could land the same way. A skit is what you'd genuinely enjoy doing. A slideshow best matches the grading rubric. What does your group choose, and why?

1. Follow your gut: Choose the live demo — a similar format worked well before, and you sense this topic will land the same way.

[icon: a spark]

2. Follow your heart: Choose the skit — it excites your group most and lets you have fun together.

[icon: theater masks]

3. Follow your mind: Choose the slideshow — it best matches the rubric and is most likely to earn a strong grade.

[icon: a clipboard with a checkmark]

`challenge questions`

How reliable is a memory of "what worked before" for a brand new topic?

Should a team's grade matter more or less than how much fun they have?

If your teammates disagreed with your gut, would you push for it anyway?

Which option requires the most trust in each other?

`reflection question`

Self-check: did your team build on each other's reasoning, or just state opinions?

### Scenario 5: The New Neighbor 19-23

A new neighbor moved in down the street. They ride their bike down the steepest hill around and always invite you to join something a little daring. Today, they ask you to try the hill with them. What do you do, and why?

1. Follow your gut: Say "maybe next time" — the way they take that hill makes your stomach flip, and something tells you to hang back and watch first.

[icon: a caution mark]

2. Follow your heart: Go for it right away — trying exciting new things is what you love, and it feels true to who you want to be.

[icon: a bike]

3. Follow your mind: Join in, but only after working out a plan — a helmet, or starting on a smaller hill — to try it more safely.

[icon: a helmet]

`challenge questions`

Is your gut protecting you here, or just reacting to something new?

Does trying it "more safely" still count as taking the risk?

What's the biggest downside of each option?

Would your answer change if a longtime friend asked instead of someone new?

`reflection question`

Your turn: has your gut ever misjudged someone before you got to know them?

### Scenario 6: The Proxima Squad 23-27

It's Proxima Trophy week, and you're already partied up with one friend. A few planetmates have made some sharp calls in earlier games this week, and something tells you they'd play well together. Your online friends are all up for teaming up, and it'd be fun either way. Or you could join a friend who's excellent at Proxima, giving your team the best shot at winning. Who do you choose, and why?

1. Follow your gut: Team up with the planetmates — something about the smart calls they made in earlier games tells you they'd be strong teammates, even though you've never played together.

[icon: a planet]

2. Follow your heart: Team up with your online friends — playing together sounds fun no matter how the game goes.

[icon: a game controller]

3. Follow your mind: Team up with your skilled friend — it gives your team the best shot at winning the trophy.

[icon: a trophy]

`challenge questions`

Does skill matter more than chemistry on a team?

What's the risk of teaming with people you've never played with?

Would your choice change if it weren't Trophy week?

Is winning the most important goal here, or is something else?

`reflection question`

Self-check: did everyone on your team get a chance to explain their choice?

### Reflection 27-28

Last question. If you could only trust ONE voice for the rest of your life, which would you pick?

- My gut.
- My heart.
- My mind.

`reflection`

Tricky, right? Most people need all three — the hard part is knowing which one to trust, and when.
