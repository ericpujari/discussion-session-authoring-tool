## Style & Guardrails Checklist

> Run every scenario through this list before calling a session finished.

**Balance test (most important):** For every option in every scenario, ask — *does this option have its own genuine, positive reason a thoughtful kid would pick it, stated with the same confidence as every other option?* If one option reads as "safe and easy" while another reads as "passive and hopeful," rewrite until they're equally legitimate. Avoid loaded words that quietly judge one option (e.g. "give up," "the responsible choice," "worst case, no big deal").

**Tone:**
- Second person, plain vocabulary — readable aloud to an 8-year-old, engaging for a 14-year-old.
- No jargon, no assumed cultural context — avoid country-specific institutions, brands, currencies, or seasons where possible.
- Light humor is welcome (silly hypotheticals, monster invasions) but never at another person's expense.

**Sensitivity:**
- Never diagnose, imply, or reference a mental health condition in a scenario, even lightly.
- Avoid scenarios that could make an actually-present, actually-quiet, or actually-left-out student in the room feel singled out or exposed.
- Handle money, family structure, and cultural norms carefully — don't assume a household income level, family shape, or religious/cultural default.
- No real named public figures. Fictional works can be referenced by title; avoid direct quotes or lyrics.

**Structure:**
- Timing is fixed — do not compress or expand scenario slots.
- Option count is consistent across the whole session (all 2, or all 3 — not mixed).
- The closing mantra ("No right or wrong answers...") belongs in the parent blurb.
